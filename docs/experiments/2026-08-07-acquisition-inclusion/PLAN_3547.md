# #3547 — a haystack deep enough to measure a long labelling session

Issue #3547. Branch `claude/acq-deep-3547`, base dev `ba0c193cc`, worktree
`/exp/sgreenberg/projects/vts-acq-3547`, study `/expscratch/sgreenberg/acq-3547`.

Follows #3319 (PR #3454, shipped `ACQUISITION_INCLUSION_OFFSET = -4`), which ran
the first deep wave this constant has ever had and could only answer half of what
it asked.

## The premise, evaluated

**Agreed, and the blocking measurement reproduces.** Recomputed from #3319's own
cells (`acq-3319-deep`, 192 cells per arm, final `t` = 400):

| arm | k | median positives | median harvest of the 150 sim positives | cells >80% | cells >90% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `prod` | 0 | 22 | 14.7% | 0.0% | 0.0% |
| `acq_m1` | -1 | 36 | 24.0% | 0.0% | 0.0% |
| `acq_m3` | -3 | 123 | **82.0%** | 53.6% | 21.9% |
| `acq_m4` | -4 | 128 | **85.3%** | 72.9% | 29.2% |

The confound is one-sided, so #3319's *win* survives it — a ceiling only the
aggressive arms reach can compress their advantage, not manufacture it. What it
destroys is the **deep** question. "Does the optimum get deeper at depth?" is a
question about the last quarter of a trajectory, and for `-3`/`-4` the last
quarter is where the pool runs out. Only the falsifier survives: the optimum does
not get *shallower*.

So the issue is right that the environment, not the harness, is the blocker.
Three corrections to how it proposes to fix it, and one design gain it does not
claim.

### 1. More positives is the wrong instruction; more positives *at fixed prevalence* is the right one

The issue says "rebuild the pile with more positives per class" and does not
mention the negative pool. Followed literally — 900 positives against
`vg_scale`'s existing 3900 negatives — prevalence goes 7.14% → 18.8% and the
landmark this whole family of studies is trying to locate,
`k* = -log2((1-pi)/pi)`, moves from **-3.71 to -2.11**.

That is a 1.6-bit shift in the answer, introduced by the fix, and it would be
invisible: the cells would look like "the same dataset, deeper". The optimum
would appear to move *shallower* with depth — H3's falsification condition —
for reasons that have nothing to do with the horizon.

`pile_config.SCALE_DEEP_N_NEG` is therefore **derived** from
`SCALE_DEEP_N_POS` against a named `SCALE_PREVALENCE`, never set beside it, and
`vg_scale_deep`'s build **asserts** the realised prevalence matches before it
embeds anything. 900 x 11700 gives 0.071429 against `vg_scale`'s 0.071429.

### 2. The scarcity is in the band split, not in Visual Genome

The issue frames depth as something a rebuild has to go and find. It does not:
the positives are already there and `vg_scale_any` is throwing them away.

`vg_scale_any` collapses `class@band` **after** designation, so its 300
positives per class are three times `vg_scale`'s 100-per-band — and 100 per band
is capped by the thinnest band anywhere in the class list. Measured
(`measure_supply.py`, the full COCO-anchored label pass, not a pilot):

* thinnest single band across all 12 classes: **138** (`bus@small`);
* band-free supply, which is what `vg_scale_any` actually needs:
  **1006** on the binding class (`stop sign`), median 1952, max 3455 (`clock`).

So the ceiling is a **selection** artefact worth 2.4x on the binding class, and
the rebuild is one changed pass over the same labels, the same COCO repair and
the same human corrections — not a re-collection.

This also settles the issue's option 1. Raising `CALIB_SIM_FRACTION` tops out at
2x (the whole pool, and no test half), pays for it in the endpoint, and changes
what that endpoint means; band-free designation is both cheaper in what it
compromises and 3x deeper. Option 1 is dominated, not merely "not free".

### 3. The deeper cell must be a new name

`vg_scale_any` carries #3115, #3196, #3287, #3290, #3318 and #3319.
`pile_config` already warns that a `vg_scale` rebuild silently changes what the
derived cell *is*. Rebuilding it deeper under the same name would make six
studies unreproducible to buy depth none of them needs. `vg_scale_deep` is a
sibling built from the same sources, and it is `on_request` so the pile's
default sweep does not silently take on five new cells (one of them a ~7 GB
`dinov3_patch` grid).

### 4. The gain the issue does not claim: one wave answers both horizons

`max_steps` reaches the simulation as a loop bound and nothing inside the loop
reads it (`voting_iterations.py:1633`, its only use). A 400-step trajectory is
therefore a strict **extension** of the 100-step one from the same seed, and the
optimum at t=100 and the optimum at t=400 can be read off **the same cells**.

This is not a saving so much as the thing that makes the question askable.
"Does the optimum move during a session?" compared across two separate waves is
a between-run contrast carrying every difference between the runs. Read off one
trajectory it is **paired within the cell** — same category, same seed, same
exemplar draw, same first 100 votes. #3319 ran two waves and could only compare
their summaries.

*Confirmed empirically before this grid was written* by comparing #3319's
shallow and deep waves at t=100 on the four arms they share
(`scripts/experiments/calibration/check_prefix_3547.py`); the plan does not rest
on reading the loop.

`vg_scale_deep` x `siglip` x `whole_image` — the shipped arm, and #2877's
environment in every respect except the pile:

|---|---:|---:|
| classes | 12 | 12 (identical list) |
| positives per class | 300 | **900** |
| shared negatives | 3900 | **11700** |
| prevalence | 0.071429 | 0.071429 |
| medias per cell | 4200 | 12600 |

| `preflight.sh` check 16b at 400 steps | REACH | **CLEAR** (450 > 400) |

900 is not a round number, it is the **deepest value all twelve classes support**

in the one comparison this study exists to make. Depth is bought until it starts
costing classes, and then it stops.

Check 16b is the codebase's own bar and it is satisfied rather than argued
around: the sim half now holds more positives than the horizon has steps.

## Hypotheses, pre-registered

**H1 — the optimum moves deeper within a session.** The likelihood-ratio frame
predicts it: the selector ranks the **unvoted** pool, whose prevalence *falls* as
positives are harvested, so `k*` moves more negative through a session. #2910's
intuition predicts the opposite (the benefit is concentrated where positives are
scarce, and deep voting ends scarcity). #3319 could not separate them.

Judged on a **difference-in-differences**, paired within the cell, rather than on
an argmin — because #3319 established that `final_cost` cannot resolve three
bits of this plateau, so an argmin over it is noise:

> `DiD = [m(deep, 400) - m(shallow, 400)] - [m(deep, 100) - m(shallow, 100)]`

for `(deep, shallow)` = `(-6, -3)` and `(-5, -3)`, on each endpoint `m`.
**H1 supported** if the DiD is negative with a CI excluding zero on at least one
of clicks-to-target, AUC or `final_cost`. **Falsified** if it is positive with a
CI excluding zero — the optimum moved *shallower*, which is #2910's reading.
A DiD indistinguishable from zero says the optimum does not move on the range
this grid covers, which retires the question rather than deferring it.

**H2 — the deep guardrail is exhaustion, not aggression.** #3319 measured `-3`'s
deep-spike incidence rising 0.5% -> **5.7% (p=0.006)** at 400 clicks, and
recorded it as an unresolved hazard because it was non-monotone (`-4` at 2.1%).
Two explanations, and the deep pile separates them:

* **H2a, aggression.** Sampling at 8:1 evidence genuinely costs guardrail at
  depth. Predicts the incidence stays around 5% in `vg_scale_deep`.
* **H2b, exhaustion.** #2790 identified deep spikes' root cause as **positive
  starvation**, and an arm that has consumed 85% of its positives is fitting a
  threshold on a pool that has almost none left. Predicts the incidence falls to
  the shallow-wave level (<=1%) once harvest drops to ~30%.

This is the first design in which that hazard is answerable rather than
re-measurable, and it is the reason the guardrail is a co-equal objective here
rather than a footnote. Note H2b would make #3319's headline hazard an
**artefact of its own ceiling** — which is worth knowing before anyone tunes
against it.

**H3 — the plateau replicates at t=100.** The deep cell's t=100 frontier should
reproduce #3319's shape: flat across `-2`..`-5`, with `prod` and `+2` resolvably
worse. This is the anchor that connects the study to the shipped constant; if it
fails, the pile change did more than add depth and **every** deep reading here is
about the dataset rather than the horizon. Reported before H1, and H1 is
withheld if H3 fails.

**Falsification arm.** `acq_p2` (`k = +2`) must make positives worse. It has
behaved in six environments. #3319's deep wave **omitted it and its analyzer
withheld the verdict in the generated report** ("Falsification arm missing —
verdict withheld"), which is exactly the control working; it is not omitted here.

## The grid

Seven arms, one wave, `CALIB_MAX_STEPS=400`:

    prod    k =  0    control; clicks-to-target is defined against its final cost
    acq_m1  k = -1    the pre-#3318 shipped value - the plateau's shallow edge
    acq_m3  k = -3    the pre-#3319 shipped value; the arm whose guardrail fired
    acq_m4  k = -4    THE SHIPPED DEFAULT (#3319)
    acq_m5  k = -5    free on binary at 100 clicks, fails region (#3319)
    acq_m6  k = -6    the plateau's measured deep edge at 100 clicks (+0.011)
    acq_p2  k = +2    FALSIFICATION

16 seeds x 12 categories = **192 cells per arm**, 1344 total. Half steps are not
run: #3319 measured them as real operating points and **decision-irrelevant**
(zero of six cost contrasts resolved), and retired them as a tuning device.

`-2` is dropped for the same reason `-1` is kept: the grid needs the plateau's
edges for H3's shape check, and one interior arm buys nothing that `-3` and `-4`
do not already carry.

Everything else is #2877's environment verbatim, at production defaults:
`CALIB_CATEGORY_MODE=prevalence`, 12 categories, `CALIB_REQUIRE_OPENING=text`,
`CALIB_CELL_ORDER=seed`, `CALIB_SAFE_THRESHOLDS=1` (the shipped fused-threshold
path), head/calibration-fraction/exclusion-floor all unset so they resolve to
production.

## Endpoints and decision rules

**Score the trajectory, not the endpoint.** #3319's own lesson, and the issue
repeats it: `final_cost` was flat across three bits while clicks-to-target
separated the plateau's edges cleanly.

* **Primary:** clicks-to-target (per-cell paired; first crossing of that cell's
  own `prod` final cost at the horizon under test) and trajectory AUC.
* **Secondary:** `final_cost` at t=100 and t=400; positives; `final_ap`.
* **Guardrail:** deep-spike trajectory rate, worst-step regret, oracle cost.
* **Reported beside every arm, as a first-class column: realised harvest.** The
  issue asks for this explicitly, and it is the number that says whether a tail
  is interpretable. An arm whose median harvest exceeds 50% has its deep
  readings reported as compressed rather than as a result.

Cost-regression tolerance stays +/-0.010, as in every study behind this constant.

## Sizing and power

* **Cost endpoints.** #3319's deep wave measured binding paired SD **0.0437**;
  n = 74 for +/-0.010. 192 cells clears it comfortably. The DiD statistic's SD
  is not known in advance — it is a difference of two within-cell differences, so
  it should be *smaller* than either, but that is an argument, not a measurement,
  and the realised SD is reported with the result.
* **The guardrail, honestly.** At `prod`'s 0.5% baseline, 192 cells expects ~1
  event, which is why #3319's `-3`-vs-`-4` non-monotonicity was uninterpretable
  and was recorded as a hazard rather than as evidence. 192 cells cannot resolve
  0.5% vs 3%. It **can** resolve H2's contrast, which is large: 5.7% vs <=1% is
  11 events against ~2, Fisher p ~ 0.01. So the guardrail is powered for the
  question this study asks of it and remains descriptive for arm-vs-arm safety
  ranking — stated here rather than discovered in the analysis.
* **Cell cost** is measured before the grid is submitted (`size` mode), never
  scaled from #3319's 10m20s: the sim half is 3x deeper and the per-step pool
  scoring runs over it.

## Amendment, 2026-09-03 09:00 EDT — the pile is sized for `-4`, not for `-6`

Recorded **during** the run, off the first ~40% of cells and **before** any
verdict was read, because it changes how H1 must be judged and a limitation
found after the answer is worth much less than one found before it.

The rebuild did what it was built to do for the arms it was sized against:
median harvest falls from #3319's 82%/85% to **18.7% (`-3`) and 34.9% (`-4`)**,
and no arm has a single cell above 80%. But `-5` and `-6` out-harvest what 900
positives was sized for and land at **55.3%** and **60.7%** — above this plan's
own 50% bar. The two arms added to probe *deeper* than the ship are themselves
mildly compressed.

Three consequences, all taken now:

1. **The DiD's primary contrast becomes `-4` vs `-3`**, the one pairing in this
   grid where neither side is compressed. `-5` and `-6` are reported beside it
   and marked.
2. **The verdict is taken on the uncompressed contrasts.** Compression caps a
   deep arm's late gains, which biases its DiD toward "no move" or "shallower"
   — one-sided, and in H1's falsification direction. So a compressed contrast
   can *corroborate* a "deeper" finding and can never *produce* one, and it is
   never the evidence for "shallower".
3. **The compression is far milder than #3319's** (60% vs 85%, and 0% of cells
   above 80% against 29%), so this is a caveat on the deepest two arms rather
   than the study-killing confound it was in #3319.

What it says about the sizing rule: 900 was chosen as the deepest value all
twelve classes support, which is a *supply* bound, and checked against
`preflight.sh` 16b, which is a *horizon* bound. Neither is an *aggression*
bound, and aggression is what actually sets harvest. A future deep grid should
size from the harvest of its DEEPEST arm, not its shipped one — filed as a
follow-up rather than left here.

## Known hazards, named in advance

* **A pilot answers questions about the MACHINE, never about the DATA**
  (`lessons/2026-09-02-one-pilot-cell-cleared-a-hazard-the-full-wave-hit.md`).
  The `size` run is used for wall time and memory only. Harvest is a property of
  the data and is sized from the full COCO-anchored supply pass, which is why
  `measure_supply.py` exists and is committed.
* **The prefix claim is load-bearing** for reading two horizons off one wave, so
  it is checked against #3319's two waves rather than asserted from the loop
  bound. If it fails, H1 falls back to a two-wave contrast and says so.
* **A new dataset is a new environment.** Nothing from #3319 transfers by
  assumption — which is what H3 is for, and why the t=100 frontier is re-run here
  rather than cited.
* **The launcher's own SLURM `analyze` job clobbered #3319's analysis.** Analyse
  to a distinct `--out` and `scancel --name=<study>-analyze` before reading
  anything.
* **`vg_scale_deep` is `on_request`.** A cell absent from `--list` is absent by
  design; it reads as `on-request`, not `MISSING`, so nobody "fixes" it by
  building 5 cells.
